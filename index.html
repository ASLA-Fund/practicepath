Graduation to license

The stretch nobody explains.

After graduation, three things run side by side: building supervised experience, passing the four sections of the L.A.R.E., and keeping your CLARB Record up to date. Here is how they fit together.

01 · Timing

You can start the exam early.

CLARB notes that most boards let you start the L.A.R.E. while you are still gaining experience. You do not have to wait until your hours are done. Check your own board, because rules differ.

See your board's rules

02 · What counts

Experience means supervised work.

Most boards want supervised experience in the four regulated areas of practice, overseen by a licensed landscape architect or a related design professional. How much you need depends on your board and your degree.

CLARB's experience guidance (free account)

03 · Paperwork

Log it as you go.

Your CLARB Record holds your verified education, experience, and exam results, and it is what your board reviews. Note your dates, supervisors, and projects as you work, so verifying them later takes minutes, not months.

About the CLARB Record

The order things usually happen

  1. 1Finish your degreeAccredited by LAAB or LAAC
  2. 2Open your CLARB RecordRight after you graduate
  3. 3Gain experienceUnder a licensed supervisor
  4. 4Pass four L.A.R.E. sectionsOften while gaining experience
  5. 5Apply to your boardCLARB sends your Record
  6. 6LicensedThen CE and renewals

Timing depends on your board, your degree, and how you schedule exams. Steps 3 and 4 often overlap.

What it costs

  • L.A.R.E., four sectionsabout $545 each
  • All four sectionsabout $2,180
  • CLARB Recordone-time fee, then yearly
  • Practice exams (optional)$25 per section
  • ASLA Associate duesdiscounted for 3 years

Exam figure is based on the CLARB Exam Success Grant, which reimburses about $545 per section. Check CLARB's fee page for current amounts.

Help paying for it

  • CLARB Exam Success Grants: about $545 back for each section you pass, if you have financial need.
  • Women of Color Licensure Advancement Program: exam fees, prep courses, and a mentor.
  • Other licensure support, including GI Bill benefits and Canadian programs.
  • Your ASLA chapter. Many offer L.A.R.E. grants.
  • Your employer. Here is how to ask.

Questions emerging professionals ask

Ask your firm

Your employer may pay for this. Ask.

Many firms cover dues, exam fees, prep, and conference time, but only when someone asks. A licensed landscape architect is worth more to a practice, so this is a business conversation, not a favor.

Things worth asking for

  • ASLA Associate dues (discounted for your first three years)
  • L.A.R.E. section fees, or reimbursement when you pass
  • Prep courses and practice exams
  • Time to attend LARE Prep Week or the ASLA Conference

    Past the finish line

    Licensed. Now what?

    Keep learning

    Track your continuing education.

    Most boards require continuing education to renew. Your CLARB Record can hold it all, add LA CES courses automatically, and print a transcript for your board. ASLA Online Learning offers courses with professional development hours.

    Track CE in your CLARB Record · ASLA Online Learning

    Stay current

    Renew on time.

    Add your license expiration dates to your CLARB Record to get renewal reminders. Each board sets its own renewal cycle and CE hours.

    Requirements by board

    Go further

    Practice in more places.

    CLARB Certification is free with an active Record and speeds up licensure in other jurisdictions. In Canada, the national reciprocity agreement covers moves between provinces.

    CLARB Certification · CSLA reciprocity

    Mentor someone one stop behind you.
